# Local-air view and held thermometer trial

The [automatic-domain follow-up](local-air-automatic-view-results.md) adds explicit `air view auto <maxAgeTicks>` selection and ownership/age-aware client history. The manual syntax below remains supported; current code uses view protocol version 4.

2026-10-26. This operator-only trial adds a sparse flow/temperature view and opts one player's held thermometers into the existing coupled query API. It does not enable production airflow, create demand, or change mounted sensor/redstone or wind gauge behavior.

## Try it

1. Start a bounded domain using the existing `/meteodyne heat air start ...` or `live ...` command. Stay within its captured air space. Preparation remains asynchronous; `status` reports progress and detailed quality.
2. Run `/meteodyne heat air view 40`. The required argument is the maximum acceptable field age in game ticks, from 0 through 40. This is a trial setting, not a selected production default.
3. Hold a `meteodyne:thermometer` in the hotbar or offhand. Its normal liquid-column display now uses the coupled temperature at the usual body point (player position plus 0.9 Y). The action bar identifies the trial and shows temperature, raw world-axis velocity, simulation-through tick, age, and cumulative maximum divergence/pressure residual when a field exists.
4. Walk through the room, open/close a supported door, or toggle a vanilla campfire. Pending, stale, occupied, and unavailable readings are named explicitly in the action bar; they clear the trial thermometer component rather than substituting an outdoor or calm reading.
5. Run `/meteodyne heat air view off` to clear experimental item readings and restore normal thermometer sampling. `/meteodyne heat air stop` stops the domain; an enabled view then reports no request until disabled or another domain starts.

The view is per player, requires permission level 2, and permits at most four simultaneous viewers. It shuts off on logout, dimension change, respawn/death, permission loss, or normal server shutdown. Session settings are not saved. It uses existing item synchronization and the existing no-reequip behavior; no new item/block IDs, resource assets, or required dependencies are added. The persistent view uses one bounded optional client payload. Trial item readings retain the simulation timestamp rather than refreshing an old frame's timestamp to now.

## Visual key and limits

The original particle implementation sampled every ten ticks on a 3x3x3 lattice; the persistent-renderer follow-up below supersedes its presentation. That lattice samples cell centers spaced two blocks apart near the player (the lowest layer is at the player's feet). Only current air samples emit vanilla dust particles to that viewer. Blue denotes -10 C or colder; red denotes 40 C or warmer, with a continuous blend between them. Calm flow is a temperature dot. Moving flow is a dotted shaft and two arrowhead points pointing downstream, including vertical motion. Length scales at 0.2 blocks per block/s and caps at 0.8 blocks; the numeric velocity is authoritative for speed.

These are local vector glyphs, not particle trajectories or a full field overlay. Sparse cell centers can miss narrow openings or partial air pockets; glyphs are not clipped to collision surfaces. Existing particles can linger briefly after a revision invalidates the sample. Normal client particle settings, distance culling, and scene contrast affect visibility. The persistent-renderer follow-up below replaces this particle implementation.

The experimental thermometer alone consumes the new field. Mounted sensors still read the established path; comparing their readings is a comparison of different models at this checkpoint. Ship/plot ventilation and transforms are not part of this world-space trial. Wind instrument horizontal/total/weather-only conventions, redstone unavailable behavior, calibration review, and production activation remain decisions/gates.

## Shared capture allowance correction

Bright's stable superflat trial had zero invalidations, attempts=1, no worker in flight and preparation=COVERAGE after five minutes or more. Its 76,829 charged cell units had consumed only 53.738 ms total snapshot time. Inspection found `prepareAllSnapshots` hard-capped local air at eight reads per tick, even when legacy thermal preparation was idle. Sky proof scans every required boundary column up to world build ceiling, so low superflat terrain requires many more reads than the high-elevation paced fixture. At nominal 20 TPS the old cap permits only 160 reads/s: 100,000 reads alone require about 10.4 minutes. This explains a long idle-looking wait independently of invalidation or worker computation.

The shared scheduler now reserves half of the 256-unit slice for whichever path goes first, lets the second path consume the remaining allowance, and lets the first reclaim unused units. First access still alternates each tick. Every call uses the same 2 ms soft deadline and combined 256-unit cap. Local air can therefore use all spare capture allowance when thermal preparation is idle; the sky proof and conservative dependency checks are unchanged. The theoretical idle-path read cap rises from eight to 256 per tick, not a measured 32x whole-simulation speedup or a guarantee of first-field latency. Tall columns, deadline contention, invalidations and admission can still delay/reject preparation.

## Fluid-state sweep omission: reproduced and corrected

Bright's subsequent status preserved the actual exception: `Incomplete thermal sweep order` immediately after successful initialization. `ThermalTransport.prepareDirectedOrder` visited each cell's air pockets and solid storage but omitted its independent fluid storage. When wind activates the directed/cached sweep path, the constructed order is shorter than the thermal-state array. The invariant then throws before the first thermal solve can finish. This is a solver traversal bug, independent of rendering, capture pacing, or boundary placement.

The new `ThermalFluidTest.directedWindSweepsIncludeFluidStatesInEveryOctantAndMatchReference` reproduced the exact exception before the fix (`build/air-fluid-sweep-red.log`). Its fixture includes both a full fluid cell and a cell with separate air, solid, and fluid states. Previous sealed/stationary fluid tests did not activate wind-directed ordering.

The correction appends each cell's represented fluid state to its directed visit order, preserving the completeness invariant. The regression checks every transport mode against the reference across all eight wind octants, at 2e-8 per-state agreement, retaining the strict thermal residual/energy checks and verifying warm fluid actually exchanges heat. ## Full-domain lattice and scale command

Solid/fluid-occupied centers are omitted. Partial cells with air away from their center may not receive a marker; this remains cell-center sampling rather than one marker for every sub-cell pocket.

Use `/meteodyne heat air view scale <blocks>` while the view is enabled: integer spacing **1 through 32 blocks**. `scale 1` restores the dense view, `scale 2` samples every second cell along all three axes, and `scale` without an argument reports the current value. Positions are anchored to the domain minimum plus half a block, so walking does not slide the lattice. Re-enabling an active view retains its scale; turning off and enabling again starts at 1. The setting is per player and not persisted. Changing it clears the prior rendered cache and takes effect at the next ten-tick update; it does not alter solver resolution, domain size, or field freshness rules.

The sample count is bounded by the existing 32,768-cell operator admission. The packet count bound is raised accordingly, and protocol version 3 requires matching updated client/server code. The largest admitted packet round-trips in a GameTest and remains below 1 MiB.
